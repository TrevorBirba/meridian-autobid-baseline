# Agreement with the original fits

The original fits are the ones the note reports. Their PLA estimates, convergence diagnostics, experiment results and
baseline metrics are exported to `results/reference/` with their values unchanged; only column names are
renamed to this package's, and the uncalibrated arm 1 baseline rows, which appeared twice, appear once. Everything below the findings is generated by
`python -m autobid_repro.compare` from those files and this package's outputs.

## Findings

**The instances are the same.** Every instance this package generates matches the original instance to 4.5e-13 on
every column, row and data seed (the CSV round trip of the original's stored frame).

**The experiments and priors are the same.** The 20 go-dark experiments match the original's effect, standard error,
true effect, withdrawn spend, iROAS, interval and prior (loc, scale) to 1e-12 or better.

**The fits read a slightly different input, and that is the whole difference.** The original pipeline did not fit
the generator's weekly values. It stored the observed series as daily values, one seventh of each week per day,
written at six decimals, and rebuilt the weeks from them. Its weekly revenue and spend therefore differ from the
generator's by at most 3.5e-6 in absolute terms (about 2e-9 relative to weekly revenue). Meridian's sampler is
deterministic in its inputs but not continuous in them: a change in the last digits sends the chains down a
different path, after which the two fits are two Monte Carlo runs of the same posterior.

Shown by refitting, not by tuning. Eight of the cells that disagree most were refitted with this package's code on
the original's exact weekly input; nothing else was changed. All eight return the original's PLA average and marginal
return to six decimals, its max R-hat to four, and its convergence flag:

**Where the difference is large, the posterior is the cause, not the code.** On performance_chasing at arms 1 and 2a
the posterior is narrow relative to the gap from truth and every cell agrees within 0.5 percent (arm 1) and
1.5 percent (arm 2a). Elsewhere a cell
can move by tens of percent or change its convergence flag. That happens where the posterior is wide relative to its
centre, where step 1's freed slope makes it multimodal, and on the 0.2 and 0.4 strength rows, which the note already
describes as jumping between two modes by data seed. There, the second fit lands in the other mode. The 1.5 percent
tolerance holds on the performance_chasing headline cells and fails on many others. Those failures measure the
Monte Carlo sensitivity of those posteriors, not a defect in the reproduction. The second-seed check below sizes
that sensitivity with this package alone.

**Table 1 against a second sampler seed.** This comparison, like every comparison with the original, is on the
plug-in returns, the only ones the original reports. The second-seed spread comes from one extra draw per cell, so it
is a rough yardstick, not a confidence bound. A cell whose two seeds happen to agree has a spread near zero, and
almost any difference then counts as beyond it. With that caveat:
- Medians move beyond the spread in 7 of 18 cells. Three of them are arm 1 none and arm 2a all_four (moves of 0.01
  to 0.08) and arm 1 all_four, which has one more converged seed here than in the original.
- The sign label or count changes in 6 cells. Every one of them also changes which seeds converged. Of the 78
  Table 1 fits that converge on both sides, one changes side of the truth: step 1, anticipatory_spend, data seed
  20260911 (3.72 here, 4.42 originally, truth 4.21). Outside Table 1, one more does: knots26 on performance_chasing,
  data seed 20260909 (4.74 here, 4.15 originally, truth 4.30).
- The headline cells do not move: arm 1 on performance_chasing reads 37.10 and 22.98 on both sides, high 5/5, and
  arm 2a 2.26 against 2.25, low 5/5.

**Long-chain test: the instability is under-sampling except in four fits with separate modes.** The fragile fits
were refitted on this package's input with 8 chains of 4,000 adaptation steps, 2,000 burn-in and 20,000 kept draws,
at sampler seeds 20260908 and 101, with nothing tuned (`results/longchain/longchain.md`). Fragile means: every data
seed of the 7 Table 1 cells whose median moved beyond the spread (arm 1 none and all_four, arm 2a all_four, step 1
none, budget_feedback, tv_bursts and performance_chasing); the 14 fits whose convergence flag differed (4 of them not
already in those cells); and arm 1 at roi_sensitivity 0.2 and 0.4 on every data seed: 49 fits, 98 long-chain fits.
The criteria were fixed before any long-chain fit was read: the seeds agree when every return differs by at most two
combined Monte Carlo standard errors; the chains agree when the split R-hat of PLA's per-draw average and marginal
return over all 16 chains is at most 1.01; a short-run value matches when within two combined standard errors of the
long chains' plug-in, the short run's error scaled from the long chains' by sqrt(8).
- **Chains agree on 45 of 49 fits.** On 41 of them the two seeds agree as well. On four the seeds differ while the
  chains agree: arm 1 all_four at data seed 20260908 by 3 percent (33.55 and 32.47, z near 6: the chains mix slowly,
  so the standard error is understated, but no chain sits apart), and three at z of 2.0 to 2.8 on one or two of the
  four returns tested (arm 1 none 20260911, arm 1 at 0.4 20260908, step 1 performance_chasing 20260910), about what
  two-standard-error tests on 196 comparisons produce by chance. These 45 are under-sampling: with long chains they
  converge to one answer.
- **The chains split on four fits, which are multiple modes.** Arm 1 at 0.2, data seed 20260908: at both seeds the
  chains divide between a low mode (chain medians near 2, the no-mechanism level) and higher ground (chain medians 3
  to 7, per-chain plug-ins 5 to 21); neither seed converges even at 160,000 draws (R-hat 1.12 and 1.26). Arm 1 at
  0.4, data seed 20260909: one chain at each seed sits apart (chain medians 9.39 and 5.74 against 2.9 to 3.8 for the
  rest). Step 1 none, data seed 20260910: one chain at sampler seed 20260908 sits at a chain median of 18.3 against
  1.6 to 1.9; all eight at seed 101 agree near 1.67. Step 1 anticipatory_spend, data seed 20260909: one chain at seed
  101 at 8.39 against 9.4 to 9.7.
- **Which mode a strength-row instance lands in is settled by long chains, except where the posterior has two.** At
  0.2, data seeds 20260909, 20260911 and 20260912 settle low (2.48, 2.14, 2.48) and 20260910 high (20.96); at 0.4,
  20260908, 20260910 and 20260912 settle high (39.86, 45.48, 36.27) and 20260911 low (3.08). Both sampler seeds agree
  on each. The short-run fits that failed to converge here (0.2 at 20260909 and 20260910, 0.4 at 20260911) were
  unmixed fits, not a different answer: the long chains match the original at 20260909 and 20260911 at 0.2 and 0.4
  respectively. 0.2 at 20260908 and 0.4 at 20260909 remain bimodal.
- **The main run and the original are both two short runs of the same posterior.** Against the long chains, both
  match on 29 fits, only the main run on 12, only the original on 8, neither on none. The Table 1 cell medians from
  the long chains (plug-in, seed 20260908 / 101) are arm 1 none 1.94 / 1.99, arm 1 all_four 38.89 / 38.80, arm 2a
  all_four 2.25 / 2.25, step 1 none 2.61 / 2.82, budget_feedback 2.22 / 2.22, tv_bursts 2.99 / 2.79,
  performance_chasing 8.75 / 8.78; main run 1.93, 38.95, 2.24, 3.04, 2.17, 2.47, 9.14; original 2.01, 38.17, 2.23,
  2.72, 2.34, 2.86, 8.78. The plug-in is itself a noisy statistic on the wide posteriors: its cap is a posterior mean
  of a right-skewed quantity, so one chain in the tail moves it (step 1 none at 20260910 carries a standard error of
  0.37 on 7.42 even at 160,000 draws).

**Estimand: per-draw returns.** The tables now report each return per posterior draw, summarized by the median and
the 5th to 95th percentile over 1,000 draws saved with every fit, instead of plugging posterior-mean cap and
posterior-median k and slope into the curve. The plug-in stays beside it, with Meridian's ROI (`Analyzer.roi()`, per
draw, over the same draws). To compute the per-draw values without changing any fit, all 280 fits (190 main, 90 at
sampler seed 101) were regenerated with the same code, settings and seeds, saving the draws: every one reproduced its
stored plug-in returns on all three channels, max R-hat, min bulk ESS, convergence flag, knots, cap, k and slope,
truth, Meridian's posterior-mean ROI and weekly decomposition exactly; none differed. The estimand section below has
the full table; in brief:
- **The per-draw median agrees with Meridian's ROI.** Its median over converged seeds is within 2.5 percent of
  Meridian's ROI median on every configuration and row except step 1 performance_chasing (1.053) and step 1 all_four
  (1.22). The remaining step 1 gap is the curve's shape: the per-draw curve averaged over the realized weekly spend,
  rather than at the mean spend, closes it (path factor 1.05 and 1.49).
- **The per-draw median sits below the plug-in wherever the posterior is wide.** On the rows without performance
  chasing it is 0.63 to 0.69 of the plug-in at arm 1 and arm 2a (arm 1 anticipatory_spend, overstated at 47, is the
  exception at 1.00) and 0.27 to 0.56 at step 1; on arm 1 performance_chasing and on the calibrated cells, where the
  posterior is narrow, it is within 5 percent (37.50 against 37.10; arm 1 strong 4.16 against 4.21). The per-draw distribution is right-skewed, so its median is below
  its mean, and the plug-in, built on the posterior-mean cap, tracks the mean: Meridian's posterior-mean ROI is within
  6 percent of the plug-in on 31 of 38 cells (not at step 1, 26 knots on performance_chasing, or arm 1 at 0.2). Mean or median of the same per-draw distribution therefore moves the
  understated cells by as much as plug-in or per-draw does.
- **Saved draws versus every kept draw.** The 1,000 saved draws carry more Monte Carlo error in the median than the
  20,000 kept draws: on arm 1 and arm 2a the per-draw median moves 5 to 9 percent between sampler seeds on the wide
  rows over the saved draws, and within 2 percent over every kept draw. Both are reported.

**On the cells that read PLA low, the posterior is the prior.** Every uncalibrated configuration puts Meridian
2.0.0's default LogNormal(0.2, 0.9) on PLA's ROI: median 1.22, mean 1.83, 90 percent interval [0.28, 5.37] (read
from the fitted model specifications; arm 2a states the same values). On every uncalibrated cell that reads PLA
low (no mechanism, budget feedback and TV bursts at arm 1, arm 2a and step 1; anticipatory spend at arm 2a and
step 1; no mechanism at every fixed knot count; 52 knots on performance_chasing; arm 2a on every row; arm 1 at 0.2),
the per-draw median is 0.94 to 1.64 times the prior's median and its 90 percent interval 0.83 to 1.77 times the
prior's width. Meridian's own ROI gives the same figures. PLA's half-saturation k shows the same: its posterior
median is 1.00 to 1.11 times its prior median on every arm 2a row, and 0.96 on arm 1 none, with interval widths
0.96 to 1.07 times the prior's; the mean weekly spend sits at about the prior's median k (x / k 0.98 to 1.07). Where
Meridian reads PLA high, the posterior has left the prior: 22 to 39 times its median at arm 1, 4 and 13 knots on
performance_chasing, arm 1 all_four and anticipatory_spend, and arm 1 at 0.4; 4.9 and 6.8 at step 1 all_four and
performance_chasing; 26 knots on performance_chasing is between (2.31). Two consequences:
- The understatement on those cells, arm 2a's included, is the prior's median against a truth of about 4.3. The data
  barely move PLA's return there; they do not estimate it low.
- The gap between the per-draw median and the plug-in on those cells is the prior's own skew: its median over its
  mean is 1.22 / 1.83 = 0.67, against per-draw over plug-in of 0.63 to 0.69.

**Calibration fixes the level, not the curvature.** The go-dark experiment replaces only PLA's ROI prior; k keeps
its default prior, and on the calibrated fits it stays there. Its posterior interval is 0.94 to 1.10 times the
prior's width on all 40, with the median at 0.85 to 1.18 times the prior's on arm 2a and on arm 1 none and 1.29 to
1.50 times on arm 1 performance_chasing. With the slope fixed at 1, marginal over average return is k / (x + k):
exactly so for the plug-in (0.49 to 0.57 over these cells), 0.46 to 0.56 per draw. Mean weekly spend sits at about
k (x / k 0.78 to 1.19), so a calibrated average near the truth comes with a marginal near half of it, while the
generator's true marginal is close to its average (4.15 against 4.29). The marginal shortfall after calibration is
the half-saturation prior's, not the data's.

**Strict convergence gate (max R-hat at most 1.01).** 24 of 56 table cells change. Most changes are a seed leaving a
cell, which turns the four-of-five rule's "low 5/5" into "indeterminate 4/4" although every kept seed is still low.

**What survives.** Values are the per-draw median, plug-in in parentheses.
- Arm 1's overstatement under performance chasing, 37.50 against 4.29 (37.10), high 5/5, under the second sampler
  seed, the strict gate, Meridian's own ROI (36.86) and both estimands.
- Arm 2a's understatement, low 5/5. It is deeper under the per-draw median: 1.53 against 4.29 (2.26), about a third
  of the truth rather than about half; Meridian's ROI median reads 1.51, its posterior mean 2.19. Under the strict

- The baseline decomposition's correlations (within 0.005) and RMSE (within 10), which do not depend on the estimand.
- The knot sweep still falls strictly: 37.50, 34.88, 27.44, 2.82, 1.26 (plug-in 37.10, 34.54, 27.80, 6.91, 1.98). It
  now crosses the truth between 13 and 26 knots instead of between 26 and 52.
- The calibrated cells: arm 1 strong 4.16, arm 2a strong 4.14 against 4.29 (4.21, 4.22), and the marginal return

**What does not survive unchanged.**
- The per-seed picture on two strength fits, arm 1 at 0.2 data seed 20260908 and at 0.4 data seed 20260909, whose
  posteriors have two modes. Every other strength fit lands in one mode reproducibly under long chains.
- Every understated cell's level: under the per-draw median the no-mechanism row reads 0.30 of the truth at arm 1
  and 0.35 at arm 2a, not about half.
- Step 1's plug-in on the rows without performance chasing, which sat well above Meridian's ROI; the per-draw median
  removes that gap.

## Data seed and sampler seed

The data seed is the generator's master seed: it fixes an instance (20260908 to 20260912). The sampler seed seeds Meridian's prior and posterior sampling. The main run uses sampler seed 20260908 for every fit: a fixed constant, not derived from the instance, numerically equal to the first data seed because the original study chose it that way. The second-seed check refits the mechanisms set (90 fits) at sampler seed 101, chosen so it cannot be mistaken for a data seed (`python -m autobid_repro --sets mechanisms --sampler-seed 101`, into `results/sampler_seed_101/`).

Only the sampler seed changes:

- sampler seed recorded on every second-seed fit: [101];
- sha256 of the generated instance (its full frame as CSV), recorded at fit time, equal to the instance regenerated now for all 40 (row, data seed) pairs: True;
- weekly revenue, PLA spend, true non-marketing sales and true PLA effect, as written by each fit, identical between the main and second-seed fits on all 90: True;
- true average and marginal return and mean weekly spend identical on every channel row: True;
- sampler seed recorded on every main-run fit: [20260908]; its instance hash equal to the instance regenerated now on all 190: True.

Medians over converged seeds; ratios of those medians, per-seed ranges of the per-fit ratio. The per-draw average agrees with Meridian's ROI when the ratio of medians is within 10% of 1.
